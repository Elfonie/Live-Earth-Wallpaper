# Data sources, licenses and credits

The code, shaders and scripts are MIT licensed (see `LICENSE`). The data files are **not**: the textures and star tables
in `app/src/main/assets` (and `web/assets`, generated by `tools/make_web.py`) are converted copies of third-party data and keep
the terms of their sources. The terms below were researched on the sources' own pages on 2026-10-21 (mostly read through a
summarising fetch tool, so re-check the key sentences before a store release). This is information, not legal advice.

All images were resized, re-encoded and in part combined (see "What was changed"). None of the sources endorses this project:
it is **not endorsed by NASA, ESA, GEBCO, the IHO or the IOC**. Do not add NASA logos.

## Non-commercial status (important)

This project is distributed **free, without ads, without a price and without donation links**. That is what the current
reading of two sources allows:

- **Gaia (ESA)** data sit in `sky_stars.bin` (via AT-HYG) and in the NASA Deep Star Maps. ESA states that Gaia data are
  distributed under **CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO** and asks for a request to `data.licences@esa.int` "prior to any commercial use",
  defined as any use that "directly or indirectly generates a financial gain" (https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia-users/license,
  https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/esdc/terms-and-conditions). ESA's DR2 credit page, on the other hand, says the data are
  "open and free to use, provided credit is given". The two statements do not agree. Ask ESA before any paid, ad-funded or
  donation-linked release (for example in a store).
- **GEBCO_08** (relief and ocean mask) has its own terms, stricter than GEBCO's current public-domain terms: reproduction "in
  derivative form for scientific research, environmental conservation, education or other non-commercial purposes is authorised
  without prior permission, providing the source material is properly credited", and "contact us first" before passing the data
  on to third parties or commercial use (GEBCO_08 documentation 2010 edition; the 2008 edition prohibits use in a commercial
  product without permission). Ask `enquiries@bodc.ac.uk`, or rebuild the relief and ocean mask from a current GEBCO grid
  (public domain, commercial use allowed), before any commercial release.

## What was changed (as the licenses require you to say)

- Day, night and Moon maps: resized to 4096x2048 (Moon 2048x1024), converted to ASTC (Android) or JPEG (PC).
- Elevation: resized, turned into a relief normal map and an ocean mask (elevation exactly 0, minus bright sea ice from the day map).
- Clouds: converted to greyscale, mip-mapped, compressed (ASTC / JPEG).
- Milky Way: the point sources were removed (grey opening) so that only the diffuse band remains; resized, gamma encoded, compressed.
- Stars: filtered to about 870,000 stars brighter than magnitude 11, positions propagated to epoch 2026.0, colours derived from the catalog colour index (B-V; for Tycho-2 stars the catalog's `ci` is BT-VT), packed into `sky_stars.bin` (see `tools/make_sky.py`).

## ShareAlike

`app/src/main/assets/sky_stars.bin` (and the copy in `web/assets`) is, most likely, Adapted Material of AT-HYG and is
therefore under **CC BY-SA 4.0**, not under the MIT license; keep it as a separate, unencrypted data file with its credit and
the generator script (`tools/make_sky.py`). The program loads it at runtime as a separate resource; in this reading the MIT code
is not a derivative of it. No Creative Commons text addresses that question directly, so this is an interpretation. Do not compile
star data into source code. Whether CC BY-SA 4.0 may be applied to the Gaia-derived columns at all (Gaia is CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO,
which is not on Creative Commons' list of BY-SA compatible licenses) is not settled by any source found.

## Methods the code builds on (no code copied)

- Precomputed atmospheric scattering: E. Bruneton and F. Neyret, 2008, and the multiple-scattering approximation of S. Hillaire, 2020. The tables come from `tools/make_atmosphere.py`, the lookup is `app/src/main/assets/atmosphere.glsl`.
- Positions of the Sun and Moon: truncated series from Jean Meeus, "Astronomical Algorithms" (chapters 25 and 47).
- ACES tone mapping curve: the common fitted approximation.

## Tools (not part of the repository)

`astcenc` (Arm, Apache-2.0) compresses the textures; Pillow, numpy, scipy, moderngl and OpenEXR are used by the scripts.
The Gradle wrapper (Apache-2.0) is included. The unit tests use JUnit 4 (EPL-1.0).
